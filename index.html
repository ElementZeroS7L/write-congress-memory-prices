AI is pricing us out of our own hardware. Tell Congress.

Memory and graphics card prices have exploded as AI data centers buy up the supply. Write your representative and senators in about two minutes: find them, add your own story, copy the letter, send it.

1Find who represents you

You have one U.S. representative and two U.S. senators. Their offices count letters from constituents, so write to your own.

Find your representative Find your senators

Each office's website has a "Contact" page with a web form. That's where your letter goes.

2Add your story

Offices give far more weight to letters in a real person's own words than to identical copies. Your details are what make this count. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

3Copy it and send it

Your letter updates as you type. Copy it, open your official's contact form, and paste it into the message box.

  • Send three times: once to your representative and once to each senator. Change the name at the top for each.
  • Pick a topic like "Technology," "Commerce" or "Consumer Protection" if the form asks for one.
  • Want to do more? Call their office and say: "I'm a constituent calling about AI-driven memory and graphics card prices. I'd like the member to push for hearings and an FTC and DOJ investigation." It takes a minute and gets tallied.

The facts behind the letter

Every claim in the letter comes from public reporting, court records or company filings.

  • J.P. Morgan projects DRAM prices will rise more than 400% from the start of 2024 to the end of 2026, adding an estimated 0.2 to 0.4 points to U.S. core inflation. source
  • AI data centers are expected to consume about 70% of all memory chips made in 2026. source
  • Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron make nearly all of the world's DRAM. Micron exited the consumer (Crucial) business entirely to focus on larger customers. source
  • A federal class action (No. 3:26-cv-06345, N.D. Cal.) alleges the three coordinated to restrict DRAM supply. On November 14, 2026, the judge denied their motion to dismiss. The allegations have not been proven. source
  • Nvidia's supply commitments climbed to $390 billion, mostly multi-year memory deals, and the same filing admits higher memory prices are hurting consumer PC demand. source
  • In mid-November 2026, every RTX 5090 listed at $1,999.99 was out of stock; the cheapest in stock was about $5,500. PCPartPicker
  • The Stopping Grinch Bots Act (S.3516 / H.R.6822) would extend the federal anti-bot law from event tickets to online retail. bill text

Much of what the public knows about GPU smuggling and the consumer side of this crisis comes from independent journalists, especially Gamers Nexus, not regulators.
